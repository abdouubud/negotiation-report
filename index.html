Negotiation Balance of power


Assess the balance of power between two parties before negotiating

Balance of power lies at the heart of every negotiation. The side that holds it sets the process and dictates the terms. Yet despite this critical stake, most negotiators don't take the time to assess the Balance of power — let alone understand how to shift it in their favor.

Misjudging the balance of power can put you at a disadvantage before the negotiation even begins.
- Lack of confidence: Give the other side too much power, and you may negotiate from a position of unnecessary weakness making concessions too early, accepting unfavorable terms, or limiting your own room to maneuver.
- High confidence: Underestimate the other side, and you may walk into the negotiation overconfident —Insufficient preparation, opening too extreme, not understanding their alternatives or failing to identify their BATNA.

The risk is not simply being wrong about who has more power. It is allowing a distorted perception of power to shape your strategy, your concessions, and ultimately the outcome.

This tool helps you close that gap. Follow the guided process and receive your personalized report at the end of the exercise.

Step 3 — Balance of Power Analysis

Following the initial subjective assessment, you will conduct a second, more analytical assessment by breaking down the balance of power into the different factors. Here are the suggested factors of power, and actions to take:

In our view, the balance of power in a negotiation comes down to five things: the leverage each side holds through their alternatives and how much they depend on the deal, how well-prepared and informed each side is going in, the strength and internal alignment of the people at the table, the relationships, reputation, and legal context surrounding the deal, and situational pressures like time and who takes the initiative. 📋
Preparation
How solid is your preparation? Have all scenarios been considered? How well-prepared is the other party?
📊
Information
How strong are your data quality, analysis, reporting, and ability to turn insight into action plans? Is data collection a manual process or automatic? What about the other side?
👥
Team
How strong are the team's negotiation skills and motivation to close the deal? How much seniority, subject-matter knowledge, and internal influence do they have?
🎯
Alignment
How aligned are you within your own organization — across hierarchical levels and with other departments? What about the other side? Are they aligned behind their strategy and actions?
♟️
BATNA
What alternative (plan B) do you have if no deal is reached? What alternative does the other side have?
⚖️
The Law
How favorable is the legal or regulatory framework to your position? Does the other side have a stronger compliance requirement that gives them more or less room to walk away?
⏱️
Time
What time pressures or deadlines are at play? Who needs this deal to close fastest? What would each party gain from a quick deal?
🏷️
Brands
How strong are your brands? How much do customers want them? How does the power of your brands influence the deal?
🚀
Initiative
Who takes the initiative in the deal? Who sets the pace and the rules of the game a. could you counter a 15-day meeting request with “can we meet in 7 days”?
🤝
Relationship history
How good is the relationship? Did past projects succeed? Have any threats actually been executed? How much trust exists between the two parties?
🌐
Networks & Influence
Do you have a single point of contact within the other organization, or multiple points of contact? How strong is your internal network and influence within the other organization?
📝
Conditionality
Are the concessions clearly defined in the contract? Are they regularly tracked with action plans? How have they been handled in the past?
🎓
Resources & Expertise
Who has more resources — financial, people, network — and how quickly can they mobilize them? What capabilities, expertise, and know-how make up your competitive advantage in the deal?
🔗
Dependency
Is there an economic dependency at play? How much weight do you carry within their portfolio? How much do they represent in yours?
